The chain rule

Unit 4, topic 5 of 7 · picks up from Tuesday
Calcu-Buddy

Welcome back, Priya. On Tuesday we worked through the product rule and you got the last two on your own — that stuck.

Today the course moves on to the chain rule. One warm-up from last week first: what is the derivative of $x^{2}\sin x$?

You

$2x\sin x + x^{2}\cos x$

Calcu-Buddy

Exactly right — and you didn’t write the formula out first this time.

So: the chain rule. It’s for a function sitting inside another function. Take $y=(3x+1)^{5}$. Which part would you call the outside, and which the inside?

You

outside is the power 5, inside is 3x+1?

Calcu-Buddy

Yes. The rule: differentiate the outside, leave the inside alone, then multiply by the derivative of the inside.

$$\frac{dy}{dx}=5(3x+1)^{4}\cdot 3$$

Your turn: $y=(x^{2}-4)^{3}$.

You

$3(x^{2}-4)^{2}$

Calcu-Buddy

You’ve got the first half. What did we do at the very end of the last one, after the $5(3x+1)^{4}$?

You

oh. multiply by the derivative of the inside. so × 2x

Calcu-Buddy

That’s it.

$$\frac{dy}{dx}=3(x^{2}-4)^{2}\cdot 2x=6x(x^{2}-4)^{2}$$

Dropping that last factor is the most common slip on this topic, and you caught it with one nudge. Ready for one where the outside isn’t a power?
